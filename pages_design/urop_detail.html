MIT-Harvard Biomedical Center UROP | Dec 2025 – March 2026

research focus: deep learning for biomedical imaging

as an undergraduate researcher at the MIT-Harvard Biomedical Center, i engineered deep learning workflows for automated coronary plaque image segmentation and OCT/OAC architecture benchmarking in Python. i collaborated closely with clinical mentors to interpret quantitative findings and adapt our modeling strategy to overcome ambiguous ground truth labels and class distributional irregularities.

architecture benchmarking

i designed and systematically benchmarked 14 deep learning architectures to evaluate relative signal extraction performance between two optical imaging modalities (OCT and OAC), implementing automated training pipelines to ensure reproducible, fair comparisons across models.

key research areas

  1. Coronary plaque image segmentation
  2. OCT vs. OAC modality comparison
  3. UNet architecture optimization & parameter sweeps
  4. Multi-objective optimization under class imbalance
  5. Cross-validated loss metric evaluation
  6. Clinical mentor collaboration & findings interpretation

model optimization

i executed structured parameter sweeps and multi-objective optimization across UNet architectures, optimizing cross-validated loss metrics to improve model generalization under severe class imbalance — a core challenge in biomedical segmentation tasks.

clinical context

working at the intersection of computer science and cardiology, this UROP gave me firsthand experience translating deep learning research into tools that support clinical decision-making — navigating real-world constraints like noisy labels, imbalanced datasets, and the need for rigorous, interpretable model evaluation.

tools & technologies

python, PyTorch, automated training pipelines, UNet and related segmentation architectures, cross-validation frameworks, and multi-objective hyperparameter optimization — applied to optical coherence tomography (OCT) and optical attenuation coefficient (OAC) imaging data.
