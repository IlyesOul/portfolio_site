MIT Nepf Lab / The Climate Project · Sept 2026–present

seeing coastal ecosystems

undergraduate research in computer vision, from noisy field imagery to reproducible perception experiments.

research focus

i’m developing Python and PyTorch pipelines for object detection and semantic segmentation in high-resolution drone imagery. the goal is to automate quadrat detection and support coastal ecosystem health monitoring.

this work brings perception into an environment where the images are rarely tidy: lighting shifts, vegetation creates clutter, and perspective changes what the model sees.

data before models

i’m developing annotation tools and quality-flagging interfaces to curate noisy field imagery. making the data easier to inspect and annotate is a key part of making the experiments useful.

training & evaluation

i use MIT’s ORCD/Engage high-performance computing resources for training and experimentation, evaluating model behavior under inconsistent lighting, environmental clutter, and varying viewpoints.

current direction

my work includes exploring classical segmentation approaches alongside fine-tuning and learning-based models. the emphasis is on building a reliable pipeline and understanding which methods hold up on field data.

the research is ongoing; the focus here is on the methods and tools in development.

why this matters to me

this project connects my interest in perception with a concrete scientific application. the challenges, imperfect observations, changing conditions, and robust evaluation, also motivate my interest in robotics and other systems that have to operate in the real world.

tools & technologies

  • Python · PyTorch
  • object detection · semantic segmentation
  • high-resolution drone imagery
  • annotation & quality-control interfaces
  • MIT HPC · ORCD/Engage
